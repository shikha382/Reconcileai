# Winning Thesis (Milestone 17)

## One-line version

ReconcileAI doesn't trust AI with money — it uses AI to investigate, and makes a completely separate deterministic system verify, decide, and prove every outcome.

## 30-second version

Finance teams reconcile payments against settlements, bank credits, and refunds from multiple sources that legitimately disagree — because of fees, timing, duplicates, and partial settlements. ReconcileAI closes that loop end to end: deterministic matching handles the clean majority instantly, and for the genuinely hard residual, AI investigates and proposes a root cause — but never decides anything on its own. An independent, deterministic verifier re-checks every AI claim with real arithmetic, a policy engine with a fixed safety precedence makes the actual call, and every decision is recorded on a tamper-evident audit trail. On our 300-record evaluation, that architecture produced a 0% unsafe auto-resolution rate, against a measured 14.67% for a naive AI-trusting alternative on the identical data.

## 60-second version

Every reconciliation product we researched — including Razorpay's own AI-branded Recon — markets "AI" with zero disclosed methodology, and none of them investigate why a mismatch happened; they only detect that one exists. ReconcileAI closes that gap directly: it ingests payments, settlements, bank transactions, refunds, and fee rules; deterministically matches the ~75% of records that are unambiguous; and for the remaining ~25%, an AI investigator proposes a structured hypothesis — a duplicate, a fee deduction, a timing delay — citing specific evidence it actually retrieved, never invented. That hypothesis is then independently re-verified with exact Decimal arithmetic against the real records, completely separately from the model that proposed it. A policy engine, with a fixed BLOCK-then-approval-then-risk-then-auto-eligibility precedence, is the only thing that ever decides an outcome — it never reads the AI's own confidence or recommendation. Every step is written to a hash-chained, append-only audit ledger, reconstructable from a single exception ID. On our 300-record, ground-truthed evaluation set, this produced a 0.0000% unsafe auto-resolution rate; a naive approach that trusted AI or fuzzy-matching directly, on the identical data, got it wrong 14.67% of the time.

## 2-minute version

Reconciliation is one of the last places finance teams still work by hand, because the easy 75% of records match on sight, but the remaining 25% require real judgment: is this a fee deduction or an error? Is this a duplicate charge or a second legitimate one? Is this settlement genuinely partial, or is money actually missing? Every product we researched in this space — Recko, Osfin, Modern Treasury, Stripe, and Razorpay's own Recon and Optimizer products — either doesn't disclose how it decides, or stops at "this looks wrong" without investigating why.

ReconcileAI is built around one non-negotiable rule: AI investigates, it never decides. Concretely: a deterministic, six-signal matching engine handles exact, tolerance-window, and fee-adjusted matches with no AI involved at all — most of the dataset never touches a model. For the residual that deterministic hypothesis-testing genuinely can't resolve, a bounded AI investigation loop proposes one structured hypothesis from a closed set of twelve possible types, citing only record IDs it actually retrieved through a small set of allow-listed tools. That hypothesis then goes through a self-challenge step and an independent verifier that re-derives the claim with exact Decimal arithmetic against the real payment, settlement, and bank-transaction records — completely separately from whatever the model said. A policy engine, with a fixed four-tier precedence (safety blocks first, then mandatory-approval rules, then risk thresholds, and only last, auto-resolution eligibility), makes the actual financial call — and it structurally never reads the AI's confidence score or its own recommended action, so there is no path for the model to talk its way into a decision. Every one of these steps — the hypothesis, the contradiction, the verification result, the policy decision — is written to an append-only, hash-chained audit ledger that can reconstruct the full story of any single exception from its ID alone, with tampering provably detectable.

We measured this, rather than asserting it: across a 300-record, ground-truthed synthetic dataset covering fourteen distinct exception categories and five deliberately adversarial cases, the system's unsafe auto-resolution rate is 0.0000%. On the identical dataset, a naive approach that fuzzy-matches records and trusts the result without an independent verification gate gets it wrong 14.67% of the time — the same category of mistake industry incumbents don't publicly disclose a defense against. That contrast, not the raw zero on its own, is the actual proof.

This isn't a claim that reconciliation itself is a novel problem, or that any single algorithm here is unprecedented — it's a disciplined, safety-first composition of known techniques, applied specifically to the one gap our research found no competitor discloses solving: investigating and explaining root cause, not just detecting that something's wrong. And when the evidence is genuinely ambiguous, the system does the thing every credible reconciliation product should do and most don't visibly commit to: it stops, and asks a human.

## The line to remember

*"ReconcileAI does not blindly trust AI with money. AI investigates. Verification challenges. Policy decides. Audit proves. And when the evidence is ambiguous, the system stops and asks a human."*
